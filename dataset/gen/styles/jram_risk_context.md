# Style guide: JRAM risk context statement (doc_type `risk_context`)

Source: CJCSM 3105.01C Fig. 3 (p. B-4), Encl. B §3.a(1)–(3). Length <= 350 words (1A.15). Directive
paragraph scheme; the six lettered paragraphs are mandatory and appear in order.

    1. Risk Context Statement: <problem set name>.
    a. Strategic Context. This assessment examines [MSR/MR] associated with [objectives, interests,
       missions, or plans], as directed by [NMS/CCP/other]. The focus is on [things of value] in a
       globally integrated context involving [stakeholders].
    b. Time Horizons. Risk will be assessed across the following time horizons, consistent with
       JSPS usage: near-term (0-3 years); mid-term (2-7 years); long-term (5-15 years).
    c. Scope and Boundaries. This assessment covers [...]. The geographic, functional, and
       operational scope includes [...]. Interdependencies with [...] will be considered where they
       materially affect risk.
    d. Roles and Responsibilities. The risk owner for this assessment is [title/organization],
       accountable for decisions regarding acceptance, avoidance, mitigation, or transfer of risk.
    e. Assumptions and Constraints. This assessment is based on the following key assumptions:
       [A1, A2 ...] and constraints: [B1 ...]. Critical dependencies that could materially change the
       risk if altered include [C1 ...]. Related risks will be grouped into [issues] to capture
       aggregation and interaction effects across [commands, functions, time horizons].
    f. Risk Tolerance and Expected Outputs. Initial risk tolerance for this problem set is
       described as [qualitative statement], subject to refinement during risk judgment and review.
       Outputs from this assessment are intended to inform [JSPS products] and/or to support
       [statutory or planning requirements].

Vocabulary: JRAM terms only (no ICD 203 likelihood words). Time horizons written exactly as above.
Acronyms spelled out at first use, glossary at the end.
