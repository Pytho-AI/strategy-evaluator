# Style guide: collection plan (doc_type `collection_plan`)

Sources: JP 2-01 Ch. III §5.a (PIR, EEI; p. III-4), §13.a–b (RFI, gap, collection requirement,
JIPCL, tracking states; pp. III-18–III-20), Fig. III-8 sample collection plan format (p. III-20),
§13.c(1)(c) LTIOV (p. III-22). Directive paragraph scheme.

Doctrine conflict resolved in favor of doctrine: JP 2-01 Fig. III-8's column headers are used
verbatim. The prompt's list (PIR, EEI, indicators, SIR, assets, LTIOV, reporting instructions)
is the content of §13.b(2) and maps into the figure's columns as shown.

    1. Collection Plan. Period covered: from DD Month YYYY to DD Month YYYY.
    a. Priority intelligence requirement (PIR) <id>: <statement>. Commander: <role>. Priority rank n.
    b. Collection plan format.
    | Priority or Other Intelligence Requirements | Indications | Specific Information Sought | Assets to Be Tasked/Resources to Be Required | Place and Time to Report | Remarks |
    | PIR id; EEI text; requirement id | indicator 1; indicator 2 | specific information requirement | routing (JIOC / JCMB / <unit> J-2) | latest time information is of value (LTIOV) DD Month YYYY; report to <role> | gap type; RFI disposition; JIPCL rank n; priority value |
    c. Status. <req id>: research | validation | submission | satisfaction (JP 2-01 §13.a).
    d. Prioritization basis. Requirements resolving a planning assumption are priced by the expected
       value of perfect information on that assumption; other gaps by (1 - confidence) x dependency degree.

No likelihood vocabulary of either scale. Acronyms: PIR, EEI, RFI, JIPCL, JIOC, JCMB, LTIOV spelled
out at first use.
